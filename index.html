Which Mars orbit for the Gateway.

Mission performance.

Mars-surface access

Descent Δv is a Hohmann-style deorbit burn to a surface-grazing periapsis (entry, descent and landing not included). A lower orbit is cheaper to descend from but may cost more elsewhere, for example in reaching the moons, so lowest is not always best overall.

Phobos and Deimos access

Coplanar circular-to-circular Hohmann transfers, approximate. Distance alone is not transfer cost.

Earth communications

Geometric line of sight only. A usable link also needs antenna pointing, power and a link budget.

Solar power and eclipse

Assumes sun-tracking arrays, 1361 W/m² scaled by the inverse square of Mars's distance from the Sun, and 80% usable battery depth.

Elliptical orbit lab.

The ellipse is compared with the three circular orbits. Scores are normalised across all four candidates using your current priority weights, so they differ from the three-orbit matrix below. Time-based values average over the whole orbit.

Priorities and decision matrix.

Each modelled metric is scaled across the three candidates (best = 100, worst = 0, higher always better), then multiplied by its normalised weight. Safety gates are not evaluated in this version.

Method and references.

How are the results calculated?
Circular orbit (R_M = 3,390 km, μ_M = 42,828 km³/s²)
  r = R_M + h             radius from Mars's centre (km)
  T = 2π √(r³/μ_M)        period (s)
  v = √(μ_M/r)            circular speed (km/s)
The Medium orbit period is computed as about 6.5 h from these equations, not the 3.9 h in the brief.

Eclipse and Earth line of sight
  Mars is a cylinder of radius R_M along the Sun (or Earth) direction.
  The station is hidden if (p·d) < 0 and |p × d| < R_M, with p the station position and d the unit vector to the Sun or Earth.
  Orbit percentages sample 360 phases at the current simulation time.

Sun and Earth positions (simplified)
  Circular coplanar heliocentric orbits: Earth 1 AU, 365.25 d; Mars 1.524 AU, 687 d.
  Starting angles are arbitrary. Mars's 25° axial tilt and orbital eccentricity are ignored.

Moons: Phobos r = 9,376 km, T = 7.65 h; Deimos r = 23,463 km, T = 30.3 h; circular, equatorial.

Transfers: Hohmann a = (r1+r2)/2, Δv = |v_t1 − v_c1| + |v_c2 − v_t2|, time = π √(a³/μ_M).
Surface descent uses only the first burn to a periapsis at R_M.
Capture Δv = √(v∞² + 2μ/r) − √(μ/r), with v∞ = 2.65 km/s (assumed).

Elliptical orbits (custom)
  a = (r_p + r_a)/2,  e = (r_a − r_p)/(r_a + r_p),  T = 2π √(a³/μ_M)
  Position: Kepler's equation M = E − e sin E (Newton iteration), periapsis on the +x axis.
  Speed: vis-viva, v = √(μ_M (2/r − 1/a)).
  Capture burns at periapsis; descent burns at apoapsis; moon transfers use whichever apsis is cheaper (Hohmann-type, coplanar, no phasing).
  Sunlight and Earth-link percentages sample 360 equal steps of mean anomaly, so they are time-weighted.
  The ellipse verdict compares weighted scores; a gain of 10+ points over the best circular orbit is a project rule of thumb, not a standard.

Limits: no inclination, perturbations, apsidal drift, plane changes or real ephemeris. Not suitable for a final engineering selection.
